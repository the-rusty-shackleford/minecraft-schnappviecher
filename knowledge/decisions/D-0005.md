# A post on the ring, kept while the victim stays in its band; routes that reach it

Rusty, on 0.1.1: the creature "gets stuck at walls because it does not find the way to an
opening", and "a target moving around flips it back and forth with no net movement". Both
reproduced on the unchanged creature before any fix (`MovementGameTests`): behind a thirty-block
wall it stood 1.3 blocks from the wall for the whole run on a one-node partial route, replanning
the same route every ten ticks; with the player turning a quarter every second it reversed five
times and ended five blocks from them.

Two causes. The stalk re-aimed every ten ticks at the point twelve blocks behind the player's
heading, so every turn of their head threw the goal to the far side of them and the creature
after it. And vanilla's search gives up after sixteen nodes per block of follow range (768), which
a wall of any length exhausts before the way round is found; the partial route ends at the wall,
and the goal, unchanged, was replanned to the same dead end forever.

## The rule

`domain.Standoff`, pure. The creature takes a **post** on a ring twelve blocks round the victim
and **keeps it while the victim stays within the ring's band** (nine to fifteen blocks of it): a
turn of the victim's head moves it nowhere; being looked at freezes it, as before; only the
victim walking out of the band makes it choose again. Choosing, it takes the candidates on the
ring **out of the victim's forward view** (sixty degrees either side of straight ahead), **nearest
to itself first**, not the one straight behind: a step round the ring, not a march to the far side.
For each candidate in that order it asks the navigation for a route, and walks the **first that
reaches**. When none reaches (the victim is indoors), it walks the route that ends nearest a post,
holds there five seconds, and only then looks again. The search budget is four times vanilla's
(3072 nodes), enough for the way round a thirty-block wall inside the follow range; the walked
length of a route is still capped by the follow range, forty-eight, so a longer detour is not
found and the creature holds at the wall instead of standing there replanning.

The approach for the theft (after the stalking delay) is unchanged: close behind the victim.

Which post a route reaches is the search's to say. Vanilla's A* overwrites a node's walked
distance on every visit and refuses nodes past the follow range, so a search that explores much
of the near side of a wall before finding the detour starves at the gap: in the booth the post
straight behind the victim (nearer) was reported short while the one to their east was reached
through the same gap, and a sixteen-fold budget changed nothing. The rule tolerates it: the first
candidate a route reaches, nearest first, and one log line per choice (`stalk from … tried …`)
naming what was tried, for the next report from the box.

## Rejected

- The view cone in the keep rule as well (leave the post when the victim turns to face it): with a
  victim turning every second the creature still reversed, chasing the cone's edge round the ring.
  A creature that stays put when looked at and moves only when its victim walks is the stalker
  Rusty described.
- A longer follow range: heavier navigation regions for every route, for detours no house needs.

## Verified

JUnit `StandoffTest` (partitions at the top). GameTests: the wall (thirty blocks, the way round
past its end, the creature reaching its post on the far side within four hundred ticks) and the
spinning player (no reversal, the ring kept). The eighteen earlier GameTests unchanged. Booth
under Complementary Unbound: a wall scene with a fake victim and the player as camera, three
shots (before it moves, rounding the wall's end, at a post beyond the wall), the post checked on
the server: south of the wall, on the ring, out of the victim's view.
